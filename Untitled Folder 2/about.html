Who We Are

Run by Parent Carers,
For Parent Carers.

We are an independent group working together with local services to improve the support on offer for children and young people with SEND in Slough.

What is a Parent Carer Forum?

A Parent Carer Forum (PCF) is a group of parents and carers of children and young people with special educational needs and disabilities (SEND) who work together with local services to improve the support on offer.

By championing lived experiences and strengthening partnerships with local providers, we help shape a more responsive, inclusive, and effective SEND landscape.

In Short...

  • 👥 Run by SEND parent carers, for parent carers

  • 🏛️ Funded by the Department for Education
  • 🧩 Part of the National Network of Parent Carer Forums
  • 📍 Exists in every local authority area in England

What is Co-production?

Co-production means parent carers and services planning and designing SEND support together, as equal partners, rather than services deciding and families being consulted afterwards.

We sit on regional and national groups and panels alongside health, education and local authority colleagues, bringing lived experience to the table at the point decisions are actually made.

Setting Clear Boundaries

While we are here to amplify voices and help improve SEND services, there are some things we don't do. This helps manage expectations and keeps our community safe.

What We Can Do

  • ✔ Represent your voice and experiences to those who plan and deliver SEND services.
  • ✔ Signpost you to the right local and national support.
  • ✔ Sit on regional and national boards as part of co-production.
  • ✔ Run events, workshops and drop-ins for parent carers.
  • ✔ Gather feedback to help shape policy and service design.

Services We Don't Provide

  • ✖ Individual advocacy or representing families at tribunals.
  • ✖ Investigating complaints or acting as a formal complaints service.
  • ✖ Counselling, therapy or peer-support groups.
  • ✖ Medical assessments, respite care or educational interventions.
  • ✖ Making decisions on behalf of families or promoting individual cases.
